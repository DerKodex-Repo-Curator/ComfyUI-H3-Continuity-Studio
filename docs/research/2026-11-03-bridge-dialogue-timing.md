## Reasoning to stress-test with the user

1. The three user ideas address different layers. First-class dialogue events describe synchronization constraints; insert/replace determines video time; linking audio determines how a particular recording follows an edit. They are not mutually exclusive global modes.
2. Inserting positive-duration frames between complete clips shifts downstream baked video. It cannot simultaneously preserve every original video frame at its original speed AND keep downstream sync to unchanged continuous original audio. At least one constraint must relax: video coverage/content, video speed, audio placement/content, or synchronization. Extra handles/regeneration can preserve important beats while changing coverage, but are not a free preservation of all original frames.
3. Merely shifting absolute dialogue labels is metadata accounting. It cannot shift speech inside an unchanged waveform or mouth movements inside rendered frames. Moving a whole clip does not require adding the bridge duration to that clip's local generation timestamps. Actual changes inside a clip require regeneration/retiming and validation; prompts do not guarantee exact realized timing.
4. A source-audio-bound dialogue event should conceptually preserve recording/source interval and map it to sequence and generation-local time. Whether a given event follows content or stays anchored to audio depends on the user's authority decision, not an unconditional downstream offset.
5. Replacing boundary coverage with a bridge can preserve downstream timing, but blindly trimming may remove speech/actions. Safe handles would need known permissible coverage, sufficient duration and endpoints at the surviving edit boundaries. Current bridge endpoints are the original tail/head, so trimming neighbors without regenerating/re-anchoring the bridge can create jumps/repetition.
6. Cutting/rippling a mixed recording moves speech and music together, may cut a word/reverb tail, and creates a duration gap needing an explicit fill decision. Crossfades smooth joins but do not restore missing musical time or guarantee speech sync. Separate dialogue/music stems permit independent policies; mixed audio cannot be treated as isolated dialogue without changing its content.
7. Continuous music/ambience and synchronized dialogue need distinct expectations. A default should not silently cut/stretch a recording, truncate a spoken line, or pretend a metadata change fixed baked media. If safe coverage cannot satisfy an audio-locked edit, report a conflict and offer a deliberate editorial remedy after the governing policy is chosen.
8. For newly planned work, allocating transition time before generation may avoid retroactive trimming; existing rendered work still needs an explicit conflict strategy. Reserve/overlap behavior is a later branch, not yet a recommendation accepted by the user.
9. Q1-Q5 imply a candidate replacement model: keep original media/takes intact and describe a bounded overlay on the existing sequence clock. Delivered replacement frames must equal the removed coverage; generation context is excluded. Anchor generation to the surviving boundaries, preserve unchanged source audio samples, and compile local prompt times through explicit source-to-sequence-to-generation mappings. This is an inferred design consequence to present for review, not an implemented or user-approved full design.
10. A source cue's intended/recorded timing and a rendered mouth-movement interval are different evidence. Speech protection must not claim accurate realized lip boundaries from `<d>` timestamps alone. Missing or stale cue/protection evidence and manually changed prompts need explicit review/conflict behavior. Technical feasibility of arbitrary chosen boundaries under native latent phase/grid constraints remains to be investigated before promising unrestricted range handles.

## Design review checklist (proposed consequences, not yet a reviewed specification)

- Exact replacement coverage must be visible before generation, including any native-grid snapping. Approved coverage cannot silently expand or change on either side of the cut. No replacement overlap with neighboring bridges or protected footage may be hidden by output assembly.
- Keep original clip media, takes and cues; store a reversible bounded replacement. Adding/removing/undoing a replacement must leave sequence duration, downstream starts and locked recordings unchanged.
- A failed or pending replacement must not make a time hole. Proposed default: original coverage/cut remains until a usable result is selected. Source, range, cue, recording or endpoint changes make dependent approvals/output stale rather than silently reusing mismatched media.
- Dialogue source intervals map through audio-lane placement to sequence time and then generation-local time including replay. Cue source times do not change when a picture-only bridge changes. Incomplete or conflicting cues/manual prompt edits need a reviewable conflict, not invented measured timing.
- Locked recordings retain their samples and placement, including across bridge regions. Generated bridge speech/music must not unexpectedly layer over the original recording. Multi-track assembly must apply one sequence clock in replacement mode rather than the current per-clip insertion shifts.
- Initial cue authoring must distinguish imported literal on-screen text from spoken dialogue. Planned Story intervals and transcripts are editable starting evidence, not certified lip-motion boundaries. Missing reliable timing must be exposed during the already-agreed replacement review.
- Existing time-inserting projects retain their meaning. Switching modes or binding fixed audio must expose timing conflicts explicitly; compatibility cannot be achieved by silently changing existing bridge semantics.
- Acceptance examples must cover several replacements, source audio starting midway through a box, source-in trimming, cross-box cues, visible speech at a join, off-screen voiceover, important silent action, exact/net duration, unsupported or too-short windows, failure/staleness, add/remove/resize/reorder/undo and old-project round trips. Geometry tests and source inspection do not establish generated transition quality.

## Decision tree and current frontier

### Settled decisions

- **Q1 accepted:** User answered "1. yes" on 2026-11-03. For an imported recording containing timed dialogue, the original recording stays continuous and determines timing by default; picture edits adapt to it and audio edits require an explicit choice. This accepts timing authority only, not an implementation, stem policy, or regeneration strategy.
- **Q2 accepted:** User answered "Yes" on 2026-11-03. Regenerating a bounded region of existing footage around the join is acceptable when necessary to fit a transition within the existing duration. Original takes remain available; recording and downstream timing stay fixed. This does not settle protected content, permissible coverage, or guarantee that a generated replacement preserves speech/actions.
- **Q3 accepted:** User answered "YEs" on 2026-11-03. Automatic bridge replacement preserves existing footage during visible on-screen speech, even if a join becomes ineligible. Explicit off-screen voiceover may continue over replacement footage. Replacing visible speech would require a deliberate override and AV review; implementation of such an override remains undecided. Realized speech intervals cannot be assumed identical to prompt timing.
- **Q4 accepted:** User answered "Yes" on 2026-11-03. Bridge creation includes reviewing and confirming the exact existing footage proposed for replacement, shown with the original audio. The user can adjust the region or keep the cut. This addresses important non-dialogue content; it does not authorize automatic semantic guesses about expendability or settle detailed UI design.
- **Q5 accepted:** User answered "Yes" on 2026-11-03. Dialogue becomes editable timeline ranges linked to the original recording, carrying words, speaker and on-screen/voiceover portrayal. Boxes derive local `<d>` instructions and bridges identify protected speech from those shared cues. Cues remain anchored to recording time when bridge pictures change. Story timings may seed cues but need checking against the recording. No automatic transcription/alignment pipeline is approved by this decision.
- **Q6 accepted:** User answered "yes for both" (Q6 and Q7) on 2026-11-03. Initial cue creation starts from supplied timed transcripts, Story data or manual entry, with editable boundaries checked against the recording. Automatic transcription and forced alignment are separate enhancements, outside the initial design scope. Existing lettering attachments are not silently converted to spoken cues.
- **Q7 accepted:** Same user answer accepts preserving explicit bridges that add time when no recording is locked to the sequence clock. With a fixed recording, replacement within existing duration is the default and cannot silently fall back to time insertion. Existing projects must not be silently reinterpreted or migrated into the new mode.
